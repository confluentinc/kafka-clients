# AI Usage Policy

We don't restrict which tools you use to write code, including AI coding assistants. Use whatever helps you work well. What matters is that you understand and stand behind what you submit. This bar applies to any contribution, AI-assisted or not.

## What we expect

**You must understand what you submit.** Before opening a PR or posting an issue, you should be able to explain what the change does, why it does it that way, and what the tradeoffs are. If you can't explain it, we encourage you to understand it better.

**You're accountable for getting it merged.** While a contribution is under review, it's yours to explain and stand behind. If it has bugs, license problems, or doesn't match the codebase's conventions, we'll work through that with you before it merges, the same way we would with any other contribution. Once it's merged, it's maintained as part of the project and we're responsible for its long-term maintenance.

**It should be your original work.** Whether written by hand or with AI assistance, don't submit code or text that includes third-party material you don't have the rights to. If an AI tool's output happens to reproduce someone else's licensed code, that's still your responsibility to catch before submitting.

**A human reply gets a human reply.** If a maintainer responds to your issue or PR, respond as yourself, and don't hand the conversation off to an unsupervised agent. Automated tooling can help you draft a response, but you're the one in the conversation. Automated replies from our systems will be clearly labeled to indicate that it was not from a human.

## Disclosure

We don't require you to disclose which tools you used. If AI assistance was substantial and relevant to understanding the change (for example, a large refactor or a generated first draft), a short note in the PR or issue description is helpful context for reviewers, but it's optional.

## Why this approach

The goal is contributions we can trust and review efficiently, not gatekeeping which tools people use. Low-effort, unreviewed "AI slop" costs maintainer time and looks the same whether it came from a tool or a person who didn't check their work. We'll treat it the same way either way: request changes, or close it with an explanation.
